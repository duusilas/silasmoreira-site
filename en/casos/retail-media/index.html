Digital retail, CDP and AdTech

From scattered data to a Retail Media experience.

How to structure a CDP, connect audiences to advertising and turn integrations into a product capability.

The business context.

Retail Media connects shoppers, advertisers and the retailer. It requires aligning the shopping experience, brand goals and operational capacity.

Data is usually scattered, and having access to it isn't enough: you need to understand it and define how it's used.

My role.

I worked on Retail Media capabilities, coordinating CDP integrations and implementing the ad server.

Along the way, I aligned sales, marketing, engineering and analytics.

The decisions behind the experience.

Start with the use case

Define the decision to support first, because each use requires different data and validations.

One clear responsibility per component

The CDP unifies data and builds segments, the ad server delivers ads and the analytics layer measures results.

Build trust in the data

Document identifiers, origin, quality and matching rules, and link purposes and preferences to profiles.

Turn the integration into an operation

Who follows up on failures, how to detect stale data and how an upstream change affects campaigns.

Explore the structure.

A six-layer reference architecture. Pick one to see what it delivers and how to check it.

Sources

Inventory of customer, transaction, catalogue and interaction data.

What it delivers

Origin, owner, purpose and frequency of each source.

How to check

Does each source have an owner and a known purpose?

Quality

Validation of structure, types, dates and fields, with error handling.

What it delivers

Ready-to-use data, with visible errors.

How to check

Are faulty records caught before they reach segments?

Identity

Linking records of the same customer, with documented rules.

What it delivers

Unified profiles without links that lack sufficient evidence.

How to check

Can you explain why two records were linked?

Segments

Translating business needs into explicit, updatable criteria.

What it delivers

Audiences with a clear definition.

How to check

Does the segment still mean the same thing after an update?

Activation

Sending only the data needed, with a contract, frequency, permissions and a kill switch.

What it delivers

Audiences delivered to the ad server in a controlled way.

How to check

Can activation be paused and resumed without losing control?

Learning

Linking delivery signals to analysis.

What it delivers

Distinguishing reach, engagement, attributed sales and incremental effect.

How to check

Do the results separate what is attributed from what is incremental?

How to turn the structure into delivery.

The same structure answers different questions: the on-site experience, campaign audiences and results evaluation.

  1. Choose a small use case

    One concrete decision to support first.

  2. Build one end-to-end connection

    From source to activation, with no hidden shortcuts.

  3. Rehearse the operation

    Failures, delays and interruptions before scaling.

  4. Learn and expand

    Measure, adjust and only then broaden.

What to watch after launch.

A CDP only makes sense when it supports a concrete use, with quality and operational responsibility.

Trust in the data

Freshness, completeness, duplicates and explaining where a profile came from.

Operational capacity

Activation success, time to availability and failure handling.

Business value

Support for decisions, the customer experience and the advertiser's evaluation.
